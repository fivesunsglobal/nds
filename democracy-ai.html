Why Democracy+AI

Civic organizations are being asked to do more with less, while losing room to act

Democracy is in decline—but decline is not destiny. At a moment when the organizations that make democratic participation possible are losing funding, space, and access, AI offers civil society a practical opportunity to rebuild capacity and regain initiative.

A democratic squeeze—and a reason to act. Freedom House and V-Dem document a sustained global democratic decline. The sudden contraction of democracy assistance has intensified that pressure: as the When Aid Fades report details, democracy organizations are reducing staff, suspending programs, or closing altogether. Credible voices are being shut out of consequential debates. The pressure is real, but it also makes new ways of working urgent.

An unequal information environment. Authoritarian uses of AI are not confined to authoritarian states: any actor can use these tools to confuse, manipulate, or manufacture the appearance of public support. Freedom House has documented the growing use of paid commenters, automated accounts, false outlets, and AI-generated content to warp public discourse, while investigations of covert influence operations show how generative AI can support false personas and multilingual messaging. AI did not create manipulation, but it increases its speed, volume, and adaptability. Democratic actors cannot afford to cede that terrain.

A strategic opening. Civil society should adopt AI now—not because every organization needs more technology, but because it can help smaller teams work around new constraints, amplify credible voices, reach more people, and explain this moment in language that resonates with their lives. AI cannot replace trusted relationships, local knowledge, courage, or judgment. It can help people with those qualities travel farther.

The adoption gap. Civil society’s caution is grounded in real concerns. Research on civil society AI adoption points to limited skills and resources, uncertainty about useful applications, privacy and security concerns, ethical questions, and a lack of organizational policy. The barrier is not simply willingness. Organizations need practical guidance that helps them move from isolated experimentation to responsible, mission-aligned practice.

The opening

Powerful tools are finally within practical reach.

AI tools have existed for years. What is new is that program managers and executive directors can now pick them up and build with them immediately—without a technical team.

Helping civic organizations understand, test, and responsibly apply these tools can rebuild capacity when it is most constrained, strengthen democratic voices, and help turn democratic decline around at scale.

FAQ

What to expect

Do I need previous AI experience?

No. The cohort is designed for AI-curious professionals who want a practical foundation. You should be comfortable using everyday web applications, but no coding or technical background is required.

Is this primarily lectures?

No. Each session combines concise explanations, live demonstrations, guided experimentation, discussion, and a practical takeaway you can continue using.

Who is the cohort for?

It is designed for executive directors, program managers, program officers, and other civic professionals responsible for programs, strategy, communications, fundraising, or organizational operations.

What is the capstone?

You will select a real challenge from your work and develop a useful output: for example, a repeatable AI-assisted workflow, a lightweight prototype, or a responsible adoption plan. The goal is something practical enough to test or use after the cohort.

How will the WhatsApp group work?

The private group will be open during the cohort for questions, reminders, resource sharing, peer exchange, and discussion between live sessions.

What if my organization handles sensitive information?

Privacy, verification, information risk, and responsible adoption are integrated throughout. The cohort will help you distinguish appropriate experiments from tasks that require stronger safeguards or should not use public AI tools.

Will sessions be recorded?

This remains to be confirmed before registration opens. The cohort is designed around live participation, and any recording policy will account for participant privacy.

Are scholarships available?

A limited number of reduced-price places will be available by request. We will include a simple request option in the final registration process.
