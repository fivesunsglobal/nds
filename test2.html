Why now

Democracy is in decline—but decline is not destiny

Representative, accountable, trustworthy governance does not renew itself. It depends on people and organizations with the freedom, resources, ideas, and tools to make democratic power real.

The democratic foundation is under pressure. Freedom House and V-Dem document a sustained global decline in political rights, civil liberties, and democratic governance. Civic space is narrowing, public trust is fraying, and institutions are struggling to respond to a faster, more fragmented world.

The organizations that make democracy possible are being constrained. Civic organizations help people organize, speak, solve problems, build communities, and hold power accountable. Yet the sudden contraction of democracy assistance is forcing many to reduce staff, suspend programs, or close, as the When Aid Fades report details. Funding is tightening just as cross-border collaboration and civic action face growing restrictions.

The information environment rewards manipulation. Authoritarian tactics are not limited to authoritarian governments: any actor can use technology and AI to confuse, divide, or manufacture the appearance of public support. Online spaces are more manipulated than ever, making it harder for credible democratic voices to reach people and earn trust.

The future remains open. We also know more than ever about how people participate, how trust is built, how networks create power, and how technology can lower barriers to action. New Democracy Studio brings those insights together with practical experience—generating ideas, convening people who can sharpen them, and testing programs that can help democracy evolve.

The possibility

Democracy can be renewed.

We have new tools to listen at scale, connect across language and distance, make institutions more responsive, and help credible voices reach people in ways that resonate with their lives.

By acting now—and pairing those tools with human judgment, trusted relationships, and a global network of allies—we can strengthen civic organizations and help restore representative, accountable, trustworthy democratic governance.

Ideas & Convening

Generating and promoting new approaches to democracy in the age of AI

New Democracy Studio creates space for critical perspectives that can change how democratic problems are understood—and what people do next.

We develop and publish analysis at the intersection of democracy, technology, and the information environment, bring people together around the questions it raises, and look for ideas that can be tested in practice.

Experts, academics, public officials, private-sector leaders, and civic actors from around the world help us challenge assumptions, compare experience across sectors and borders, and see possibilities that would be difficult to find alone.

Convening is how we generate and road-test ideas. The strongest can become programs, partnerships, or experiments tested in context.
